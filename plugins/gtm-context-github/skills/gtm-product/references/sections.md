# The six sections of a GTM context layer

Every later system (outbound, inbound, content) reads these six sections before it does anything. Each section answers one question. If a section can't answer its question in one or two screens, it is too long: move examples further down.

| Section | The question it answers | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Product & offer | What do we sell, to whom, for how much, and what result does it give? | Website, sales deck, the user's own words |
| ICP | Who is worth our time, who is not, and how do we tell them apart from a profile? | Best existing customers, or competitors' customers when there are none |
| Competitors | Who else sells the same result to the same buyers, and how are we different? | Public websites, pricing pages, case studies |
| Messaging & customer language | How do buyers describe the problem and the result, in their own words? | Call transcripts, email threads, DMs, reviews |
| Objections | What stops people from buying, how often, and what answer works? | Calls, emails, DMs, an existing list |
| Customers | Who already buys, which segment they belong to, and which logos we may show? | CRM, invoices, a pasted list, the user's memory |

## Quality bar for every section

1. **Summary first.** The first 3 to 5 lines say the most important thing. A busy agent that reads only the summary should still act correctly.
2. **Specific beats complete.** "Founders of 5 to 30 person B2B agencies in the UK" is useful. "SMBs" is not.
3. **Their words, not ours.** Customer language and objections are quoted verbatim, with a source tag, never paraphrased into marketing copy.
4. **Evidence or a flag.** Every claim either has a source (a customer, a call, a page) or is marked `(guess, confirm)`.
5. **Every offer in scope.** When the business sells several things and more than one is in scope, each section covers each of them: one product section per offer, ICP cards per offer, value props per offer, and customers and objections tagged with their offer.
6. **Skip what doesn't apply.** A solo consultant doesn't need five personas. Leave a section short rather than padding it.
6. **Dated.** Each page shows when it was last updated, so a stale page is easy to spot.

## B2B vs B2C

- **B2B:** the ICP describes companies (industry, size, stage, geography, tools) and then the people who buy inside them (role, seniority, what they own).
- **B2C:** the ICP describes people by situation and trigger (life moment, problem, budget, where they spend time), not by demographics alone.
- **Agencies, consultants and B2B2C:** capture two layers. Layer 1 is the client who pays you. Layer 2 is your client's customer, because your results depend on reaching them.

## Update rhythm

- Product, ICP, competitors, messaging: monthly, or after a big launch or pricing change.
- Objections: every few weeks, from new calls and emails.
- Customers: whenever someone new buys or gives logo permission.

The student's scheduled update task, created at the end of the build, does this on a schedule: banks every week, a full check of offers and pages once a month. A section older than its rhythm shows up as "stale" in the update report.
