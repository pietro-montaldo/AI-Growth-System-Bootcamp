# Formats

Each format draws on a different source. Fill the `{{}}` parts from the profile. The defaults below hold unless the profile says otherwise.

## Reply to an email

Source: their replies. These are the most reliable numbers in the profile.

- Answer the question in the first or second line. Context comes after, if it's needed at all.
- Length should match the incoming email and their reply median ({{N}} words). Never write more than they would.
- If the email asks several things, answer in the order asked. Use their usual layout (short paragraphs or numbered), never bold headers.
- If you'd need a fact you don't have (a date, price, or yes or no), leave a [placeholder] and flag it. Never invent one.
- Greeting and sign-off come from the register table for this reader.
- {{their specific reply habits}}

## Cold email

Source: their cold emails if there are 10 or more, otherwise their call openings and how they describe the offer.

- Target {{N}} words. Hard ceiling {{p90}}. Most cold emails that work are under 100 words.
- One real, specific reason for writing to this person. It has to be something you can name: their role, a trigger event, a post with what it actually said, a mutual connection. If you don't have one, ask {{FIRSTNAME}} or use [reason], and never make one up.
- Say what they do in their words: "{{offer line}}".
- Make one small ask, in their phrasing: "{{ask}}".
- Subject: {{their pattern, e.g. lowercase, 2-4 words, no clickbait}}.
- No compliments you can't back up, no "I came across your profile", no "I hope you're well" unless the profile says they use it.

## Follow-up

Source: their follow-ups.

- Add something new each time: an answer, a resource, a specific idea for their situation, or a simple yes or no question. A bare bump only if that's what they do.
- {{N}} words. Shorter on every nudge.
- Third or later nudge: {{their tone, e.g. "light, gives an easy out"}}.
- After a call: what we agreed, what I'm sending, and the next step with a date. Use their words from the call when the transcript is available.

## New email to someone they know

Source: their `new` emails, a client, partner or past lead they write to first.

- {{N}} words. Usually shorter and warmer than a cold email, because the context is shared.
- Open with why you're writing now, in one line. No reintroduction.
- Greeting and sign-off from the register table for this reader.

## LinkedIn post

Source: things they say often on calls and webinars, plus the structure of posts they confirmed they wrote.

- Start with the point: an opinion, a mistake people make, or a number. No "Let's talk about", no question hooks unless that's their pattern.
- One idea per post, with one comparison or story from "Things they say often" and one rule of thumb the reader can use today.
- First person, from what they actually did or saw. No "experts say".
- Line breaks: {{their pattern}}. No emoji bullets, no "Agree?" ending, no hashtag stacks unless the profile says so.
- Any mention of their offer goes at the end, short, and only if they asked for it.
- End with one or two normal, full sentences. Never a punchy pair of short lines or a two-line moral, and never a sweeping "everyone X, nobody Y" contrast. Both read as AI even when the words came from the person.

## DM and connection note

Source: cold emails, cut in half.

- Connection note: under 300 characters. One reason, no pitch.
- DM: {{N}} words max. Write it like a text to a colleague. Use their casual register from the peer row.
- If they engaged with the person's content, quote the actual post, not a summary.

## Comment

- Add one thing: an example from their experience, a disagreement, or a sharper version of the point. Never "Great post!" or a restatement of the post.
- 1 to 3 sentences. Their peer register.
