# Reading for voice

The fingerprint gives the form. This guide covers what only reading catches: the moves. Write down quotes as you go, since every trait in the profile needs one.

## Part A: emails

Read 30 to 40 human emails. Start with cold emails, then follow-ups, then replies to prospects, then replies to existing clients. For each question below, note a pattern only if it shows up in at least 3 emails, and keep one short quote for it.

### Openings
- Do they greet, and how does that change with how well they know the person?
- First sentence: the answer, a thanks, context, or a question?
- How do they reference the last contact? "Great talking yesterday", "Following up on Tuesday", or nothing at all?

### Getting to the point
- How many sentences pass before the actual point or ask?
- Do they explain why before what, or the other way round?
- How do they deliver bad news, a no, or a price increase? Softened, or straight with a reason?

### Commercial moves
- **Cold**: what's the hook? A mutual connection, a trigger event, a sharp observation about their business, or a straight offer? How specific is it? How do they describe what they do: one line, a result, a client name?
- **The ask**: how big is the ask in a first message? A call, a question, permission to send something? How do they word it? Copy the exact phrasing.
- **Follow-ups**: how many words? Do they add something new, or just bump? What tone do they take on the third nudge?
- **Price and proposals**: options or one number? Do they justify the price? How do they hand over the decision?
- **Objections and pushback**: do they concede, reframe, or hold firm? Do they use a question to handle it?
- **Next step**: a specific date and time, or "let me know"? Do they offer a calendar link?
- **Closing loops**: how do they say thanks after a deal or a referral?

### Relationship register
Compare the same person's emails to a stranger, a prospect mid-deal, a long-time client, and a peer or partner. Note what changes: greeting, length, jokes, sign-off, how much they explain. This becomes the register table in the profile.

### Texture
- Humor: present? What kind: self-deprecating, dry, none?
- Opinions: do they commit ("I'd do X") or offer options?
- Their own words for their own work, like "workshop" or "session", "clients" or "customers". The finished skill must use their nouns.
- Language mixing: do they slip in words from another language? On purpose?
- Typos and casual habits they keep: lowercase "i", no period at the end, "thx". Keep the deliberate ones and drop real typos.

### What they never do
Scan for absences across the whole set: no exclamation marks, no "I hope you're well", never "per my last email", never bullet points, never "Dear". Absences are as defining as habits.

## Part B: calls

Use this template per batch of calls. Subagents return exactly this.

```
CALLS: <files>, <talk share each>

1. How they open a call (first 60 seconds): quote
2. How they describe what they do / their offer, in their words: 2-3 quotes
3. Explanations: comparisons and analogies they use (quote each, note the concept)
4. Rules of thumb and tests they give ("if X, then Y"): quotes
5. Stories and examples they repeat (one line each + where)
6. How they handle objections (price, timing, "we'll do it in-house", "send me info"): quote + move used
7. How they talk about price and options: quotes
8. How they ask for the next step / close: quotes
9. Opinions they state strongly: quotes
10. How they admit limits / say "I don't know": quotes
11. Questions they ask prospects (discovery questions in their words): list
12. Verbal habits: fillers, check-ins ("right?", "okay?"), signposting ("number one..."), with rough frequency from speech.json
13. Humor and warmth: quotes
14. Anything that would sound wrong written down (run-ons, tangents, false starts)
```

### Spoken to written
Spoken voice copied straight onto the page reads messy. For each verbal habit, decide whether to keep, translate or cut, and record it in the profile's table:

- Keep the moves: analogies, rules of thumb, opinions, admitting limits, signposting.
- Translate the rhythm: run-ons become short sentences, "okay?" check-ins become at most one per piece, imagined dialogue becomes a short quoted line.
- Cut the filler: um, like, basically, you know, et cetera, false starts, apologies for tangents.
- Don't turn spoken one-liners into punchy written closers. A line that works mid-call ("the judgment is mine") becomes an AI-sounding aphorism when it's set alone at the end of a post. The same goes for sweeping "everyone X, nobody Y" contrasts. Keep the idea, and put it inside a normal, full sentence. (Both showed up in blind tests with real users.)

### Posts come from the calls
A person's best posts are usually something they've said ten times on calls. Collect the opinions, rules of thumb and stories from part B into the profile's "Things they say often" list. Those are ready-made post material, already in their voice.
